# I — Historical ground

## 1. The technical object

The Alfonsine Tables were among the major practical astronomical instruments of later medieval and early modern Europe. They supplied numerical material used to compute the positions of the Sun, Moon and planets and circulated together with **canons**: procedural texts explaining how the tables were to be used.

The broader `zīj` tradition provides the deeper historical type: an astronomical handbook in which mathematical astronomy, numerical tables and rules/procedures of calculation are composed into a practitioner's instrument.

The history is itself technically important. Alfonsine tables and parameters were copied, adapted, corrected and recomputed; later manuscripts and computational work can preserve intermediate derivations as well as final tables. The instrument was therefore both stable enough to support practice and historical enough to be revised when calculation, parameters or observations demanded it.

Useful external historical sources:

- Oxford Cabinet, **Tabule astronomice Alfonsi Regis (Venice, 1492)** — practical astronomical use, planetary/Sun/Moon computation, circulation and correction: <https://www.cabinet.ox.ac.uk/node/9821>
- José Chabás & Bernard R. Goldstein, **The Alfonsine Tables of Toledo** — Castilian canons, astronomical content and diffusion: <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-94-017-0213-3>
- **Alfonsine Astronomy: Computational Practices** — procedures expressed through texts, tables, diagrams and instruments: <https://www.brepols.net/products/IS-9782503610771-1>
- Study of Lewis Caerleon's Alfonsine computational practice — recomputation, intermediate tables and derivational evidence: <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00407-023-00324-y>

These sources establish the historical Figure. Epi source establishes the Epi relation.

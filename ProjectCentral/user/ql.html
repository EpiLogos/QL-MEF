The executable product

QL is a formal computation and reading product, available through the ql command and Rust libraries. Its native command surface exposes kernel capabilities and address operations, the twelve-lens registry, Context Frame names, Vāk source lookup and bounded context, service negotiation and a verification command.

The libraries provide the deeper typed contracts: positions and faces, addresses and pair families, whole and partial shapes, MEF coordinates and readings, external Context Frame mappings, provider dispatch and client adapters. These contracts let another program request a precise operation while preserving its own objects.

A human can begin with ql capabilities --json to inspect the exposed surface. ql mef lenses lists perspectives; ql context-frame list lists the current named contextual forms. The command is a doorway into the existing native owners, with version information available alongside the result.

One field of structural operations

The kernel gives a position and a face separate identities. Same-position conjugacy relates a direct position to its prime counterpart; complement relates a position to five minus that position on the same face. Those operations can share a vertex while remaining different relations. Their identities and provenance let a client tell them apart.

Three pair families, A, B and C, select defined pairs. Within a selected pair, D1 records the direct pair, D2 a one-sided conjugate expansion and D3 the completed conjugate square. The resulting shape retains the chosen family and pair index, including where two constructions reach the same unordered vertices.

The shape contract includes partial constellations, 4×4 and 6×6 fields, and the relational sixfold 6+6′. An address locates a place at which a relation can be examined. The application supplies the meaning of the content at that address, making the field useful across different subjects.

Lenses, contextual forms and source language

MEF supplies twelve lenses, six Day and six Night, each with an internal sixfold articulation. A lens is a perspective over an identified subject. Several lenses can reveal different relations of that subject while preserving a common identity for comparison.

Context Frames describe canonical cuts of a sixfold relation. The structural operator records what is selected and unselected. An external-target reading additionally carries the mapping by which the caller placed its own material in the field, and can disclose an exact, partial, ambiguous or absent match.

The Vāk registry exposes source-defined entries and their references. The CLI can locate an entry or return its neighbourhood at bounded depth. A researcher receives the source revision and coordinates needed to return to the formulation, rather than an anonymous paraphrase.
